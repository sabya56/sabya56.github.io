Setup

How to set up an Android phone as a dash cam

An old phone makes a genuinely good dash cam. It already has the camera, the GPS, the accelerometer and the storage that a purpose-built unit charges you for. What it does not have out of the box is loop recording, impact detection, or a camera that keeps running with the screen off — which is the part DashCam adds.

  1. Mount the phone facing the road A suction or vent mount at windscreen height, camera pointing forward. Landscape gives you more of the road per frame; portrait gives you more of the lane ahead. Both are supported, and the orientation can be pinned so a bump does not rotate it.
  2. Give it power Recording video with GPS running will outpace the battery on a long drive, so run a cable from the car's USB socket. DashCam can start recording the moment that charger connects and stop when you unplug, which means the dash cam turns itself on with the car.
  3. Set a storage budget Tell the app how much space it may use. It writes in short segments and, once the budget is full, the oldest clip makes room for the newest — so it runs every day without ever needing to be cleared out. The space is reserved up front, so another app cannot take it mid-drive.
  4. Turn on the overlay you want burned in Date, time, speed and GPS coordinates can be written into the video file itself rather than drawn over the preview. This is what makes phone footage read as dash cam footage: the data is in the picture, not in a sidecar file that gets lost.
  5. Tap Record and put the phone down The camera keeps running with the screen off, and carries on while you use Maps or take a call. If the G-sensor detects an impact, that clip and the one before it are moved out of the loop and never deleted.
Auto-start on Android 14 and later. From Android 14, an app cannot start a camera service from the background. Granting DashCam "Display over other apps" is what lets the charger and Bluetooth triggers work completely hands-free. Without it you get a single "tap to start" notification instead — one tap, but it always works.
What it does

Built around the clip you actually need

Most of a dash cam's footage is worthless and one clip of it is not. Everything here is arranged around making sure the app is still holding that one clip weeks later.

Loop recording

Short segments inside a budget you set. Oldest deleted first, so it never fills the phone and never stops for lack of space.

Impact detection

The G-sensor measures deviation from a gravity baseline, so it is not fooled by mount angle. A knock saves the clip and the run-up to it.

Incidents are never deleted

Protected clips are held out of the loop permanently — not overwritten to make room, not trimmed under storage pressure.

Records with the screen off

The camera is bound to a foreground service, not to the UI, so the session survives the screen turning off and app switching.

Speed and GPS in the frame

Burned into the recorded file per frame, not overlaid on the preview. Choose which fields show, which edge, and km/h or mph.

Hands-free start

Begin recording when the car charger connects, when your stereo pairs over Bluetooth, or after the phone finishes booting.

Floating preview

A small draggable window so you can see and stop the recording without leaving Maps.

Works with no signal

Recording, retention, overlays and playback are entirely on-device. A tunnel or a dead zone changes nothing.
